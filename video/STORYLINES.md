# Storylines

You asked for the finished videos, so I built the recommendation and kept the other two here in case you prefer one.

## A. One Wind Left (built)

**Device and form:** a character carries the story, shot as a miniature film inside the real game engine, with each cut caused by the action before it.

Baron Rust, the one toy nobody ever wound, steals the Golden Key, and every toy in the workshop winds down, except Tock, who has one wind left. The outro pays it off: Tock wins, and then winds the Baron too.

- **First two seconds:** Tock on the bench at dusk, his key turning, as he hops.
- **Beats:** dusk → the key → Baron wakes → the snatch → everything winds down → one click → run through the levels → title.
- **Outro beats:** the Baron falls apart → his hat lands on Tock → the key turns → the toys wake → "Even him." → the end.
- **Look:** the game's own renders at 1080p with anti-aliasing on, with brass-gold titles like the game's logo.
- **Sound:** the game's own sound effects on the actions. There's no music yet, because no music source is available here.

Picked because a stranger follows it with the words covered, it explains why the game has a clock, and the ending changes how the villain's first line feels.

## B. The Toymaker's Ledger

A storybook. Pages of a craftsman's repair ledger turn, with drawn sketches of Tock and the Baron coming to life in ink. Warm and quiet, but it would look less like the game you actually play.

## C. Broadcast from the Workbench

A mock nature documentary. A deadpan on-screen narrator "observes" gear-beetles and the rare rubber duck in the wild until Baron Rust ruins the shot. The funniest of the three, but it would need a voiceover to work.
