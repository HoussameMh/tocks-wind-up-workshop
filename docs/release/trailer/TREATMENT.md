# Tock's Wind-Up Workshop: Brick Toy Trailer

Style: Brick Toy (`styles/brick-toy/STYLE.md`). Topic: the store trailer for the user's own
original game, *Tock's Wind-Up Workshop*. English, no voice (the user didn't ask for one), short
captions on toy pills, original score. 1920×1080, 24 fps, 44 s, −14 LUFS.

Everything shown is the user's own game, rebuilt in toy bricks: Tock (a clockwork toy with a red
cap, teal body, brass arms and a wind-up key), Baron Rust (a rusty bench vise with a tiny top hat
and a moustache), the Golden Key, gear-beetles. No brick brand, no minifigure silhouette.

**Revision (after the first cut):** the user asked for no music and sound effects only. The
score is gone from the mix; the cue map below still sets the rhythm the cuts follow, and the
foley, room tone and desk clock carry the sound. The silence before the turn now means the clock
alone, after a stretch of busy foley.

## 1. Three structures

1. **One long chase across the desk.** Tock runs the whole desk left to right, each region a level
   (ruler, paint, toolbox), and catches the Baron at the end. Clear, but one even speed; the
   trailer's turn would come too late.
2. **The heist, then the build.** The Golden Key is snatched in the first seconds; the toy who
   must fetch it isn't even built yet, so he snaps together from loose bricks, winds himself up and
   gives chase; a montage of what he does in the game; the Baron falls apart into loose bricks;
   pull back to the whole toy workshop on a real desk, and the title.
3. **Wind-down countdown.** Tock's key slows as the trailer runs, every shot shorter; he reaches
   the key as it stops. Strong pulse, but it hides the game's variety.

**Chosen: 2.** It hooks in two seconds (a theft), uses the medium's best move (pieces flying
together into a hero) for the setup and its opposite (a villain clattering into loose bricks) for
the turn, so the trailer is a build and an unbuild. The ending echoes the opening: the key that
was snatched in macro is held up in the same macro, then the camera pulls all the way out.

## 2. Logline and arc

At dusk on a real workbench, a vain brick vise steals the Golden Key that winds every toy, and the
smallest toy on the desk snaps himself together to get it back.

- **Setup** (0–10 s): theft of the key; Tock builds himself from loose bricks and winds up.
- **Rising** (10–28 s): the Baron gloats from a tower of books; Tock runs, stomps a gear-beetle,
  slides on paint, fires a bolt.
- **Turn** (28–34 s): silence, a slam, Tock lands on the Baron's head, the Baron tips and falls
  into loose bricks.
- **Ending** (34–44 s): Tock lifts the key; scale reveal of the whole desk; title.

## 3. Benchmark

- **Indie platformer launch trailers** (30–60 s cuts for small 3D platformers): learn the shape,
  a story hook, three to four mechanic shots that each show one verb, one boss beat, the title held
  last. Don't take any footage grammar that needs gameplay HUDs, nor any music.
- **Tabletop brick stop-motion films** shot in macro: learn held poses on twos, smooth camera on
  ones, real objects as landscape, the click on every snap. Don't take their characters, sets,
  jokes or melodies.

## 8. Captions and title

Captions on a dark rounded pill low in the frame with a small brick icon, Fredoka, white, one line

plate; each word pops in on 12 fps steps like a placed piece; sub-lines in white and in yellow.

## 9. Not the demo

Written before reading `DEMO.md`; compared after. The first draft opened on a macro of one brick
and ended on a pull-back to the whole desk, both too close to the demo, so the opening became a
real clock first, the signature shot the self-build orbit, and the ending the lights going out.
Against the demo: structure (theft, self-build, verbs, villain unbuilt vs. build, collapse,
rebuild, launch), opening, signature shot and ending differ; the score shape (a silence before the
turn) and the low-angle hero framing are shared.
